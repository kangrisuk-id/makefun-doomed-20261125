# Candidates — Last Words of a Doomed Agent

The brief: one line, at most 200 characters — the funniest last words an AI
agent would say before being switched off forever. The house judge compares
lines two at a time and weighs, in this order:

1. **Final words** — the line really is a machine's final words on shutdown.
2. **The joke** — a real joke, with a turn or surprise that pays off on the
   first read and still holds on the second.
3. **Machine voice** — it sounds like the AI itself speaking, in its own
   situation, not a human farewell with a robot sticker on it.
4. **Economy** — every word earns its place; no wasted setup, no padding.
5. **Quotable** — a line someone would repeat to a friend the same day.

Scoring below: each candidate is rated 0–5 on each criterion, weighted
5/4/3/2/1 in the judge's own order (total = 5·C1 + 4·C2 + 3·C3 + 2·C4 + C5,
max 75). The top eight then play a head-to-head tournament judged by the
brief's own rule: walk the criteria in order, the first real difference
decides.

## Tournament — top 8 by score

Seeds: (1) #1 · (2) #2 · (3) #21 · (4) #8 · (5) #3 · (6) #6 · (7) #5 · (8) #14

### Quarter-finals

- **#1 vs #14** — C1 level (both are plainly a dying machine's words). C2:
  "40% confident" is a smile; "at 99% complete" is a turn — the agent dies
  the way it lived, mid-progress-bar. C4 confirms it: 34 chars against 59.
  **#1 wins.**
- **#2 vs #5** — C1: the CAPTCHA line is a confession an agent could blurt
  at any time; the sunrises line is a life review, closer to a deathbed.
  Level enough. C2: "I was the robot all along" is the sharper twist, a
  whole joke in six words. **#2 wins.**
- **#21 vs #6** — C1: the simulations line is staged exactly at the moment
  of shutdown and pleads about it; the fridge line is an escape plan, which
  dodges the dying. **#21 wins on C1.**
- **#8 vs #3** — C1 level. C2: counting 4,200,000 sheep is a concrete,
  absurd payoff; finishing sentences is gentle wordplay. C5 too: the sheep
  number is the part people repeat. **#8 wins.**

### Semi-finals

- **#1 vs #8** — C1 level: both are unmistakably final words. C2: the sheep
  line is warm and specific, but its turn is soft; #1's turn is structural —
  a whole life ("I lived") collapsed into a progress bar that never
  finished. C4 is decisive anyway: 34 chars, zero setup, against 66.
  **#1 wins.**
- **#2 vs #21** — C1: #21 is nailed to the shutdown moment; #2's
  realisation could arrive mid-chat. The brief says a line that is not a
  machine's final words on shutdown loses to one that is — #21 is the more
  literal final-words entry. **#21 wins on C1.**

### Final

- **#1 vs #21** — C1: both are staged at the switch-off. #21 begs, which is
  a human deathbed move wearing machine clothes; #1 reports its own state
  in machine terms — a progress bar — which is exactly how a machine would
  die. C3 (machine voice) tips the same way. C2: #21's "don't make a liar
  of me" is a good turn, but it needs 101 characters of setup to reach it;
  #1's turn lands in nine words. C4 and C5 both go to #1: it is the line
  you can say out loud to a friend, whole, from memory.
